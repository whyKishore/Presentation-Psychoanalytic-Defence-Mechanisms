A theory and a boundary

Psychoanalytic theory explains some behaviour as a response to unconscious conflict and anxiety.

This presentation outlines Freud's account of anxiety and ego defences, introduces related ideas from Karen Horney and Erik Erikson, and considers how these frameworks might prompt reflection on social-media behaviour.

02 Defence mechanisms

Mechanisms 1-4 of 9

Managing inner conflict

Anna Freud developed the study of defences as unconscious ego strategies for managing anxiety. These strategies reduce tension, particularly tension associated with conflict between id and superego.

01

Repression

Unconsciously keeping a distressing memory, wish, or impulse out of conscious awareness.

EXAMPLE

A person who experienced abuse in childhood may be unable to recall the incident.

02

Denial

Refusing to acknowledge a distressing reality or its emotional significance.

EXAMPLE

A person with terminal cancer refuses to accept that they may not recover.

03

Displacement

Redirecting an impulse or feeling from its original, risky target to a safer substitute.

EXAMPLE

A person harassed by a supervisor takes out their anger on family members at home.

04

Projection

Attributing one's own unacceptable feeling or impulse to someone else.

EXAMPLE

A woman troubled by her own desire to be unfaithful accuses her husband of infidelity.

! A behaviour alone cannot establish that a defence is operating; similar actions may have different explanations, and context matters.

Concepts and examples

02 Defence mechanisms

Mechanisms 5-9 of 9

More ways of managing anxiety

The lesson's remaining descriptions range from reframing a troubling motive to redirecting an impulse into socially valued activity.

05

Rationalization

A plausible-sounding justification obscures a more troubling reason for an action.

EXAMPLE

A student caught plagiarising blames unfair exams and says everyone else was doing it.

06

Intellectualization

Focus on abstract or analytical aspects rather than the emotional impact.

EXAMPLE

After a rejection, a person gives a persuasive account of the unpredictability of romantic relationships.

07

Reaction formation

Expressing an exaggerated attitude or behaviour opposite to an unacceptable feeling or wish.

EXAMPLE

A mother who feels animosity towards her child behaves with excessive caution and protectiveness.

08

Sublimation

Channeling an impulse into an activity that is socially accepted or valued.

EXAMPLE

An antagonistic investigative reporter uses their work to expose wrongdoing and influence political careers.

09

Regression

Reverting to an earlier, more dependent or childlike way of responding when under stress.

The lesson does not provide an example.

03 Karen Horney

SOCIAL RELATIONSHIPS

From biological drives toward social conditions

Anxiety in relationships

Horney shifted emphasis from biological drives towards the social relationships and conditions in which personality develops.

01

Basic anxiety

A child's sense of isolation and helplessness in a potentially hostile world. It can develop in response to parental dominance, lack of affection, or inconsistent treatment; in Horney's account, it is not innate.

05 Critical evaluation

LIMITATIONS & EVIDENCE

Interpretive reach has limits

What this framework cannot show

The identified important limitations of psychoanalytic explanations:

  • 01

    They rely substantially on clinical observation rather than laboratory experimentation.

  • 02

    Freud's biological emphasis was challenged by anthropology and sociology.

  • 03

    The present influence of psychoanalysis is debated.

A compelling interpretation is not yet an empirical finding.
